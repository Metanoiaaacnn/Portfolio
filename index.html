02 / TASK ONE

Co-curricular & community engagement

Participation in a flag ceremony at Tisa Elementary School in Cebu City.

HOST SCHOOLTisa Elementary School
ACTIVITYFlag Ceremony (Grade 3 – Codilla)
DATENovember 16, 2026
LOCATIONF. Llamas, Cebu City

About the school and activity

My experience of participating in a school activity at a public elementary school was both meaningful and educational. Upon arriving at Tisa Elementary School, one of the first things I noticed was how huge the school was. It had many classrooms, students, and facilities that created a lively learning environment. Seeing the school and its students gave me a better appreciation of the environment where young learners begin developing their knowledge, values, and discipline.

During my visit, I had the opportunity to witness the school's flag ceremony. What caught my attention was that, unlike the usual flag ceremonies I was familiar with, which are commonly conducted outside on the school grounds, this one was held inside the classrooms. The students stood beside their desks, facing forward and participating in the ceremony. At first, I found this different and somewhat unexpected because I was used to seeing students gather together in an open area for the flag ceremony. However, seeing how the activity was still properly conducted inside the classroom made me realize that the importance of an activity does not depend on where it takes place, but on the purpose and values it teaches.

People involved

The people I observed during the activity also made the experience memorable. The elementary students, despite their young age, showed participation and respect throughout the ceremony. Their teachers also played an important role in guiding and maintaining order among the learners. It reminded me that teachers do not only teach academic lessons but also help develop discipline, responsibility, respect, and good citizenship among their students.

One-page reflection

One of my biggest realizations from this experience is that education is not limited to textbooks, lessons, or classroom discussions. Even simple school activities, such as a flag ceremony, can teach important values that students can carry with them as they grow. I also realized that schools can adapt their activities to different situations without losing their meaning and purpose. What matters most is that students understand why they are participating and the values behind the activity.

Overall, this experience gave me a different perspective on the school environment and the responsibilities of teachers in shaping young learners. It allowed me to appreciate the efforts of educators and the importance of providing students with opportunities to develop not only academically but also as responsible individuals. As someone learning about the teaching profession, this experience reminded me that being a teacher is not just about delivering lessons but also about creating meaningful experiences that help shape the character and future of every learner.

03 / TASK TWO

Teacher interview

A conversation with Mrs. Judeth Gonzales, an English teacher at Lawaan National High School, about professional practice and the realities of teaching.

INTERVIEW RESPONDENT

Mrs. Judeth Gonzales

English Teacher

Lawaan National High School30 years of serviceNovember 16, 2026

The respondent has 30 years of teaching service.

Recorded face-to-face interview · 14:58Within the 15-minute limit.

INTERVIEW GUIDE & RESPONSES

Key discussion points

01

Milestones of the teaching journey

She began her career at Don Aniceto Soriano National High School before transferring to Lawaan National High School. One of her proudest experiences was helping a struggling Grade 7 learner improve their reading skills through summer remedial classes. She was also promoted from Teacher 5 to Teacher 6.

02

Personal philosophy of teaching

Her teaching philosophy is based on Progressivism, which emphasizes learning through experience and actual practice. She believes students learn best through hands-on activities, collaboration, inquiry, and active participation.

03

Teacher ethics

She believes teachers must serve as good role models by practicing fairness, honesty, decency, and integrity. She also emphasized maintaining professional boundaries with students and treating everyone impartially.

04

Student discipline and punishment

She describes her approach as firm but gentle. She establishes classroom rules with students, uses verbal reminders instead of corporal punishment, and promotes discipline through consistent routines, cleanliness, and shared responsibility.

05

DepEd laws and policies

She highlighted the importance of free and accessible basic education and the ARAL initiative, which helps learners improve their literacy and numeracy skills. As a reading coordinator, she recognizes the importance of providing additional support to struggling readers.

06

Best and most challenging experiences

Her greatest happiness comes from seeing former students succeed and remember her through visits and appreciation. She finds it challenging when parents do not cooperate and when teachers handle additional responsibilities such as feeding programs, deworming, and paperwork.

07

Working with school heads and officials

She described her relationship with the school head as positive and respectful. She believes disagreements should be addressed through proper communication, understanding, and professionalism rather than through social media or unnecessary conflict.

My interview reflection

This interview helped me understand that teaching involves more than delivering lessons. It requires patience, dedication, emotional strength, and genuine concern for learners. I was particularly inspired by how the teacher helped a struggling student improve their reading skills through consistent guidance.

I also learned the importance of fairness, classroom discipline, and hands-on learning. As a future educator, I hope to apply these lessons by creating an inclusive classroom, supporting learners who need extra help, and maintaining good relationships with parents and colleagues.

Most importantly, I realized that teaching comes with challenges, but a teacher must remain committed, professional, and passionate about helping students grow.

Teacher hiring & assessment results

TWO-PAGE NARRATIVE REPORT

My experience at the Division Office

On November 16, 2026, at approximately 10:30 in the morning, I visited the DepEd Division Office of Cebu City with my bloc mates. Since the office was located near Cebu Normal University (CNU), we were able to reach it by walking. The purpose of our visit was to examine the public announcements and documents displayed on the bulletin board and gain a clearer understanding of the work and responsibilities carried out by the Department of Education.

When we arrived, we first observed the bulletin board positioned outside the office building. Although it did not contain many posted notices, the information presented was significant. The announcements covered matters such as the hiring and assessment of teachers, as well as government procurement activities. We read the available documents carefully to learn about their purpose and relevance.

One of the notices we examined was the Elementary Comparative Assessment Result (CAR) for School Year 2026–2027, together with the CAR Registry of Qualified Applicants for the same school year. We also saw applicant registries for Kindergarten, Junior High School, and Senior High School. These documents are connected to the process of evaluating and selecting individuals who are applying for teaching positions. The Comparative Assessment Result provides information about the applicants' assessment, while the registry lists those who qualified according to the required standards. Making these results publicly available helps promote transparency in the recruitment process.

We also found announcements about the procurement of supplies and services for the Schools Division of Cebu City. One notice involved the early procurement of commonly used supplies and equipment for Fiscal Year 2027. This showed that the division makes preparations in advance to ensure that schools and offices have the materials they need. Planning purchases early can help prevent delays and make sure that essential resources are available when needed. Other posted notices focused on the procurement of annual janitorial services for the DepEd Cebu City Division Office, as well as the purchase and delivery of janitorial and electrical supplies for the schools division. These services and materials help keep offices and school facilities clean, organized, safe, and usable.

Another announcement was about the procurement of annual security services for the division office. This process is intended to provide security personnel who can help maintain safety and protect the people and property within the premises. We also noticed a Supplemental Bid Bulletin, identified as Addendum No./Corrigendum No. 1, and documents marked Initial Evaluation Results (IER). These are part of the formal bidding and procurement process followed by government offices. A supplemental bid bulletin communicates added details, explanations, or revisions to earlier bidding instructions, while the Initial Evaluation Results show the outcome of the first review of the documents submitted by bidders.

During our observation, we also saw several public school teachers entering and leaving the building. Their presence gave us an idea of how the division office serves as a place where teachers can attend to work-related matters, submit requirements, and seek assistance. As future educators, we realized that the work of the Department of Education is not limited to teaching and learning inside classrooms. It also includes administrative responsibilities such as recruiting qualified teachers, providing necessary resources, managing services, and supporting the operations of schools. These tasks are important because they help create the conditions that allow teachers to perform their duties and learners to receive quality education.

Overall, our visit to the DepEd Division Office of Cebu City was informative and worthwhile. Although our observation was mainly focused on the documents posted outside the building, the announcements gave us a glimpse of the many processes that support the education system. The experience helped us understand that a well-functioning school system depends not only on classroom teachers but also on the offices, personnel, and procedures working behind the scenes.

Key learnings

  • Teacher recruitment uses assessment results and registries to make the selection process transparent and standards-based.
  • Early procurement and service contracts help ensure that schools and offices have the supplies, cleanliness, safety, and security they need.
  • Supplemental Bid Bulletins and Initial Evaluation Results are part of a fair and formal government bidding process.
  • The Division Office supports teachers and schools behind the scenes; quality education depends on both classroom teaching and administrative work.

MY TEACHING PHILOSOPHY

“Teaching is more than simply sharing knowledge; it is the responsibility of guiding, inspiring, and empowering learners to become confident, responsible, and independent individuals.”

I believe that every student has unique abilities, experiences, and potential that deserve to be recognized and developed. As a future educator, my goal is to create a learning environment where students feel respected, included, encouraged, and motivated to discover their capabilities. Effective teaching requires patience, understanding, creativity, and adaptability. I will use varied strategies, activities, discussions, and real-life experiences to make learning meaningful and enjoyable, and I will encourage students to ask questions, express their ideas, think critically, and learn from their mistakes rather than fear failure.

Empowering learners

I will recognize each learner's unique abilities and create a classroom where students feel respected, included, and confident to express themselves. I want every learner to discover their capabilities and reach their full potential.

Pedagogical practice

I will combine clear instruction with varied strategies, activities, discussions, and real-life experiences so that learning is meaningful, inclusive, and enjoyable. I will encourage questions, critical thinking, and learning from mistakes.

Ethical principles

I will practice fairness, honesty, respect, compassion, and professionalism. I will treat every learner equally, protect their dignity, keep confidential information private, and model the values I want students to develop.

Core values

Empowerment · Inclusivity · Patience · Integrity · Lifelong Learning

Professional goal

To become a teacher who measures success not only through grades and academic achievements but also through the growth, confidence, values, and positive changes of every learner.
